SOURCES & METHODOLOGY

Simple, consistent presentation rules

Move: latest complete daily observation versus the prior complete observation. Price assets and FX are shown in percentage change; government-bond yield moves are shown in basis points.

Trend: five complete daily observations from 14 Sep to 18 Sep, shown on separate y-axes for each benchmark. Endpoint labels sit beside the final point so they do not cover the line.

Data convention: benchmark closes and trend series use a single Investing.com historical-close convention for consistency. S&P 500 and macro/market context were cross-checked against Associated Press (AP) and Reuters; Fed and BOJ decisions were checked against official central-bank releases. When vendors display different intraday, contract or end-of-day conventions, the dashboard keeps one consistent historical-close series across headline, chart and narrative.

Cutoff: common cross-asset market-data cutoff is 18 Sep 2026. Crypto also uses the 18 Sep daily close to keep the comparison aligned with the Friday close in stocks, FX, commodities and bonds.

AI REFLECTION

How AI supported the dashboard

AI tools used and why
  • ChatGPT: Structured the dashboard, refined the market commentary, generated the HTML/SVG layout, and checked consistency across sections.
  • Web research: Verified market closes, policy decisions, event dates, and source consistency before publication.
  • Python: Checked calculations, percentage and basis-point moves, sign direction, chart coordinates, and endpoint labels.
Prompts used
  • Update the five asset classes using the latest fully completed market day and keep the benchmark set unchanged.
  • Explain the key driver behind each market move clearly and connect it with the latest cross-asset background.
  • Check data consistency, dates, units, signs, axis scales, endpoint labels, and cross-asset logic before publishing.
Challenges
  • Different market close times: Stocks, FX, commodities, bonds, and crypto do not share exactly the same trading schedule, so the dashboard needs one consistent cutoff.
  • Different data conventions: Vendors can use different close, contract, and quote conventions; one consistent historical series is used for each benchmark.
  • Different units and scales: Bond yield moves are shown in basis points while other assets use percentages, and each trend chart needs its own y-axis.
  • Clarity vs. accuracy: The explanations must stay concise without oversimplifying the relationship between policy, yields, inflation, and risk assets.
